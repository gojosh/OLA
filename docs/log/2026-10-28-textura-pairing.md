# 2026-10-28 — Which capitals go with the plate 9 textura

**Agent/tool:** Claude Code (Opus 5.5)

## What happened
- **The question.** Josh: "let's tackle the textura face at the bottom of the same page. Would you say its intended to be combined with any particular capital face in the 4 typography pages? Or perhaps just with these Lombard capitals we've just finished?"
- **The plates compared.**
  - Plate 9: Lombardic capitals, with a flourished textura minuscule below them, all in red.
  - Plate 10: large Gothic capitals, with a second, smaller Lombardic set below them.
  - Plate 11: a large textura minuscule, with no capitals.
  - Plate 12: a condensed textura minuscule, decorated Gothic capitals, and a smaller, rounder minuscule.
- **Pugin's own account.** The Glossary's entry *Inscriptions* dates every alphabet on the four plates, and says the letters were selected "with a view to their imitation at the present time". Plate 9's capitals are of the 13th–14th centuries; its small alphabet is of the late 15th. Plate 10's capitals are of the 15th century; its small alphabet is earlier. Plates 11 and 12 are of the 16th century. Found by searching the Internet Archive's OCR text; ⚑ verify the wording and page against the page images.
- **The answer given.** Plate 9 sets two alphabets a century or more apart, as models, not as a pair. By date, the textura's capitals are plate 10's Gothic capitals. The Lombardic capitals belong above it as initials, which was their medieval role. Proposed: build the minuscule first, set it with Lombardic initials meanwhile, and decide its capitals then (new Q36).
