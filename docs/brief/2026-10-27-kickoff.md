# Kickoff brief — 2026-10-27

## Message 1

This is the start of a big project - overhauling the website of Our Lady of the Atonement. You'll see some stuff in the included directory, but that is just fodder, not a starting point. The ambition for this site is extremely high - ideally, the best catholic church website ever made. That's all! ;) Please help me organize things, and set up the project such that other LLMs can onboard as needed along the way easily - this is about properly structuring artifacts and conventions.

Four ground-setting things to kick us off:

1. Audiences for the website

* Catholic Inquirers (is this a Parish I would like to visit or join?)
* Inquirers (non-Catholic and non-Christian - what is this place?)
* Parishioners (this is an important resource for my parish life)

     ⁃ Church ministries/leadership (this is where we record things and organize)
This possibly implies a "remembered"  state of the site, which recognizes returning parishioners, and tailors their experience for members.

We'll need to keep these audiences in mind as we create the information architecture. I'll also want your advice on this breakdown, and any group I perhaps should be thinking about, that I'm not thinking about.

2. Aesthetic

English-Catholic. I'm using that term very specifically, because our heritage is not just Anglican which has been welcomed back into the Catholic Church. That is absolutely true, but I'm digging deeper here. We're grounding into the English Catholic tradition, reconnecting to the church that existed prior to Henry the VIII's devastation to the Catholic faith in England.
That means tying to the medieval, in a thread of continuity to the present. Continuity will be a key word here. We are richly drawing on the whole catholic tradition, but with a particularly English focus. Gothic is our architectural inheritance, and we relish it. Pugin represents a particularly vibrant touchstone, as he is a bridging character - fully English, deeply Gothic, devout convert back into the Catholic faith - he looms large in our aesthetic landscape. But we are also deeply American. What might be American Gothic? That's a question that hangs in my imagination, waiting to manifest.

3. Tech

We are in the glory days of what can be done on the web. Nothing is off limits. We should stretch technology to its absolute limit if necessary for realizing a beautiful vision, while developing a iron-clad rigor - a meaning-imposing logic, to bring about a timeless unity and continuity. We may use WebGL with advanced shaders, we may use extensive animated SVG rendering, everything is on the table, but always, ALWAYS in service of some specific and known good for the user, never just because.

4. Information Architecture

Once we have some basic block and tackle in place for the project, we'll start by thinking about the information architecture.

My first stab at simplifying from today's navigation was in pursuit of a much easier-to-understand navigation at first glance. This doesn't take too much into account in terms of audience segmentation, FYI.
