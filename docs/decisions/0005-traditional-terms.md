# 0005 — Traditional terms, and the high-church term when in doubt

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-27
- **Decided by:** Josh Goforth

## Context
The first section design (`docs/sections/sacraments.md`) used a mix of current catechetical vocabulary ("Holy Eucharist," "Anointing," "Marriage") and traditional names. Josh: "You should use traditional terms throughout - our parish is traditional - when in doubt, use the high-church term when it comes to technical things - communion vs eucharist, for example." ([brief](../brief/2026-10-27-sacraments-and-terms.md)) Clarified the same day: "I'm not asking us to use antiquated language, rather to use traditional terms. 'Holy Unction' is archaic, and no longer in use… Half the time, we'll pick the older term, some are no longer in use." ([brief](../brief/2026-10-27-terms-clarification.md))

## Decision
- The site and the project docs use traditional terms for technical things: the names of rites, sacraments, seasons, feasts, objects, places, and offices.
- When in doubt, use the high-church term.
- **Traditional, not antiquated.** The term must still be in living use: in the Church's current books, at OLA, or in how traditional Catholics speak today. Names the Church has retired are not revived ("Holy Unction," "Extreme Unction").
- Where the parish already has a traditional name (Holy Matrimony, Said Mass, Sung Mass), use the parish's.
- Plain English labels ("Visit," "Becoming Catholic," "funeral") are not technical terms and stay plain.
- The working list lives in [`docs/glossary.md`](../glossary.md) under "Usage." Additions follow these rules; rows marked ⚑ Josh are open until he settles them (as Q29 settled Anointing of the Sick and Mattins and Evensong).

## Consequences
- "Holy Communion" for what is received, "the Blessed Sacrament" for the Sacrament reserved and adored. "Eucharist" appears only in quotations and in the titles of cited sources.
- The calendar engine and the *Today* page name seasons and Sundays by the Ordinariate calendar ("after Trinity," not "in Ordinary Time").
- Some traditional terms are unfamiliar to visitors. A proposed accommodation, not yet ratified: where someone in a hurry or from outside the Church has to find something, the everyday word may appear beside the traditional one, never instead of it, and an unfamiliar term is explained briefly the first time a visitor meets it.
- Every agent writing copy or docs checks the usage list first (AGENTS.md §5).
