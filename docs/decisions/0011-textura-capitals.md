# 0011 — The plate 9 textura: minuscule first, then plate 10's capitals

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-28
- **Decided by:** Josh Goforth

## Context
Plate 9 sets Pugin's Lombardic capitals above a flourished textura minuscule. Josh asked whether the textura was meant to go with any particular capitals on the four alphabet plates, or with the Lombardic capitals. Pugin's own entry *Inscriptions* in the Glossary dates the plate's capitals to the 13th–14th centuries and its small alphabet to the late 15th. He dates plate 10's large capitals to the 15th century, and says the letters on these plates were selected "with a view to their imitation at the present time" (see `reference/pugin-glossary/README.md`). So the plate is a pair of models, not a matched set. Lombardic letters served medieval books as initials, above the text.

## Decision
- Build the plate 9 textura minuscule first, as its own family.
- Meanwhile, set it with the Lombardic capitals as initials.
- Afterwards, give it capitals: plate 10's 15th-century Gothic capitals, the capitals of its own period.

Josh: "Go with your proposal: build the plate 9 textura minuscule first, set it with Lombardic initials meanwhile, and give it plate 10's 15th-century capitals afterwards."

## Consequences
- The textura family starts without capitals. Its proofs set it under Lombardic initials.
- Plate 10's large capitals become the next alphabet after the minuscule. Its smaller Lombardic set is separate work.
- The textura follows decision 0008: built from a simulated broad nib, with Pugin's flourished forms as alternates on plain forms drawn from the same parts.
