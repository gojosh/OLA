## Working proposals

### 1. The premise

General explanation of the sacraments is now abundant. An AI assistant, the *Catechism* online, and any number of Catholic sites will tell you what Baptism is. Anything we write that could sit on any Catholic website, or come back as an AI answer, is low value for us to write and costly to keep accurate. The old site's long catechetical pages with embedded video playlists (2019–2023) are exactly that kind of content.

So the test for every sentence in this section: **could only Our Lady of the Atonement say this?**

### 2. What only we can say *(accepted; #4 deferred)*

Five things. They're also roughly what an AI cannot give.

1. **Here: the facts of receiving it at OLA.** Who can receive it here, the steps, the timeline, the preparation, the documents, any cost, and who to call. Precise, current, and owned by a named person. An assistant can't know these. When someone asks one "how do I get married at Our Lady of the Atonement?", our page is what it should be quoting, so these facts should be plain, structured, and dated (see §6).

2. **How we celebrate it: the rite in this tradition.** *Divine Worship* carries the English Catholic inheritance into each sacrament: the words, the music, the place. The vows of Holy Matrimony are the clearest example. "To have and to hold from this day forward, for better for worse, for richer for poorer, in sickness and in health…" was already said *in English* in the pre-Reformation Sarum rite, passed through the Prayer Book, and is said at OLA today ⚑ verify the *Divine Worship* wording. That is continuity a couple can hear in their own mouths. Nobody else can show what these rites are like here.

3. **A person.** Who will walk with you, by role and name, and how to reach them. An assistant answers questions; it can't accompany anyone. The page's real job is to get people to a priest or a staff member, gently, especially in the hard cases: a previous marriage, a dying parent, a baby in the NICU, thirty years away from Confession.

4. **Witness.** *Deferred.* Josh: "we can skip #4 for now." The idea, for when it returns: people of this parish on what a sacrament or their reception meant to them, with their consent. OLA began as eighteen converts from the Episcopal Church.

5. **Why, briefly, in our voice.** One or two paragraphs on what the Church believes happens in the sacrament, written well and pointing to the *Catechism* paragraphs for depth. We don't rewrite the *Catechism*; we cite it precisely and send people to it.

**What we don't do:** long catechetical essays, video playlists, link lists, apologetics.

### 3. The shape of a sacrament page

Ordered by the reader's urgency. Someone arranging a Requiem needs the phone number before the theology; an inquirer will scroll.

1. **The one thing to do.** For example, "To arrange a baptism, call [CONTACT]." The person or role, shown at once.
2. **Here.** Steps, requirements, and timeline, as structured fields, so they can't drift and can appear in feeds and search results.
3. **Hard cases.** The situations that need a conversation with a priest, named plainly and with compassion. "If either of you has been married before…" "If your child is in the hospital…"
4. **What it will be like.** The rite at OLA: a few of its words (⚑ licensing; see §7), its music, a photograph of the actual place (the font, the altar, the confessional).
5. **Why it matters.** Short, in our voice, with *Catechism* references.

*(Witness would follow, if it returns.)*

### 4. How the section is organized

Use the Church's own grouping, from the *Catechism*, rather than a list of seven. The landing page presents the sacraments as one picture: they accompany a person from birth to death. That is also the most helpful way for someone outside the Church to understand them.

| Group | Sacraments and rites | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiation** | Holy Baptism · Confirmation · Holy Communion · **Becoming Catholic** | Holy Communion links to Worship → Holy Mass. Becoming Catholic covers instruction and reception into the Church, for the unbaptized and for baptized Christians. |
| **Healing** | Confession · Anointing of the Sick | Confession also lives under Worship, where its times are. |
| **At the service of communion** | Holy Matrimony · Holy Orders | The *Catechism*'s heading. "Communion" here means the communion of the Church, not Holy Communion; the site label may need different words. |
| **And at the end** | **Funerals:** the Requiem and the Burial of the Dead | Not a sacrament: the Church's care for the dying and the dead. No page exists today. |

### 5. Sacrament by sacrament: what's particular to us

Starting points for writing, not content. Every policy below is ⚑ verify with the parish.

| **Becoming Catholic** | Seekers; Anglicans and other Protestants; the unbaptized | Instruction (OCIA, or OLA's own name for it ⚑ verify); reception of baptized Christians; **who can be enrolled in the Ordinariate** (the criteria are specific and often misunderstood: exactly what AI gets wrong) ⚑ verify | Reception into the Church at Holy Mass, in this tradition | A previous marriage (needs review before reception); former Anglican clergy |

### 6. The AI angle, beyond not duplicating

- **Be the source.** Assistants will answer questions about OLA from what they can read. The *Here* facts should be published as plain text plus structured data, with a "last confirmed" date, so the answers people get elsewhere are correct.
- **Point, don't compete.** For doctrine, link precisely to the *Catechism* rather than paraphrasing at length.
- **Possible later:** a guide on the site that answers only from OLA's own pages and always hands off to a person. That would need an Effect Ledger entry, and a very good reason to exist.
